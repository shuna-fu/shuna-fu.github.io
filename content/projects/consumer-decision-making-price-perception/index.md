This project examined how tourism environments shape consumers’ perceptions and acceptance of product prices. Combining behavioral measures with event-related potentials (ERPs), the study investigated how attraction type and price information jointly influence consumer decision-making across different stages of cognitive processing.

## Status

Completed (Nov 2018 – Jun 2019)

## Role

Project Manager · Undergraduate Thesis

## Organization

EEG Lab, Jianghan University, China

## Research Questions

- How does tourist attraction type influence consumers’ perceptions and acceptance of product prices?
- How do consumers respond to price variations across different tourism contexts?
- At what stages of cognitive processing do contextual and price information influence consumer decision-making?

## Research Methods

- Experimental design
- EEG / Event-Related Potentials (ERP)
- Behavioral experiments
- Questionnaire measures
- E-Prime programming
- Behavioral and electrophysiological data analysis

## My Contributions

- Designed the experimental paradigm and procedures to investigate context-dependent differences in consumer price perception.
- Programmed the behavioral and ERP experiment using E-Prime.
- Managed and processed more than 200 behavioral and ERP datasets.
- Conducted behavioral and electrophysiological data collection and analyses using SPSS, MATLAB, and Excel.
- Contributed to the development of the project from thesis research to peer-reviewed publication.

## Key Findings

- Tourist attraction type influenced consumers’ perceptions and acceptance of product prices.
- Consumers were generally willing to accept higher product prices within tourist attractions.
- ERP evidence suggested that price information captured cognitive attention during earlier stages of processing, whereas attraction type and price information jointly influenced decision-making during later stages.
- Consumers showed different levels of price sensitivity and tolerance for price increases across attraction contexts.
